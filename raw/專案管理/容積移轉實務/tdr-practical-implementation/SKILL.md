# TDR Practical Implementation & Market Controversies

## Overview
This skill outlines the practical, legal, and market challenges of executing Transfer of Development Rights (TDR) in Taiwan. It separates these practical considerations from the strict legal definitions found in building regulations. It is essential when advising clients on the actual transactional risks, valuations, and property rights securing (保全) associated with TDR.

## Key Controversies & Market Dynamics

### 1. Legal Nature & Property Securing (保全)
- **Special Sacrifice Claim Right**: TDR is legally classified as a "right to claim for special sacrifice" (特別犧牲請求權), distinct from standard Civil Code real rights (物權) and claims (債權).
- **Registration Issue**: TDR cannot be categorized on land titles (地籍謄本), making it impossible to officially register and legally secure (保全) in a private transaction using standard civil procedures.

### 2. TDR Bank (容積銀行) Challenges
Advocacy for a centralized TDR Bank faces significant hurdles:
- **Government Purchase Dilemma**: If the government buys TDR, how is it set up and secured?
- **Theoretical Paradox**: Once the original owner is financially compensated, their "special sacrifice" is resolved. It becomes legally ambiguous whether the government's newly acquired TDR can still be called a "special sacrifice claim right."

### 3. Market Structure & Valuation (估價)
- **Public Facility TDR**: The government acts as a monopoly seller. Official appraisals are effectively mandatory, as developers have no alternative source.
- **Cultural Heritage TDR**: Involves private-to-private transactions. Professional appraisals (估價) serve merely as a reference. Neither buyer nor seller is forced to accept the valuation, meaning it completely lacks transactional compulsion.
